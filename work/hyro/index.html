Getting a regulated crypto exchange to launch when the critical path runs through other people

HYRO is building Ghana's first regulated digital-asset exchange inside the Ghana SEC sandbox. My job was to turn a web of identity, payment, vendor and regulatory dependencies into a plan the team could build against.

Role
Project manager
Team

01The problem

An exchange looks like trading and swaps. Underneath, it's registration, authentication, identity verification, AML, wallets, payments, fiat on- and off-ramp and settlement. Most of those depend on outside providers and regulators.

The deciding constraint: nobody can trade until they're verified. So the critical path wasn't the trading engine. It was compliant onboarding, which depended on a reliable identity provider and regulatory clearance.

02Evidence

HYRO is one of 11 firms admitted to Ghana's SEC regulatory sandbox under the Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025. That sets clear expectations for identity, AML and data protection before launch.

Reviewing the external vendor's MVP proposal showed where scope and quality fell short of what a regulated product needs, especially around deployment, responsibilities and third-party providers.

I structured the work into a 60+ item backlog across 10 workstreams, including KYC, identity verification, authentication, payments, on/off-ramp, settlement, wallets, frontend, backend, vendor integrations and data protection. That made the dependencies visible to everyone.

03Decisions and trade-offs

Verify against the national ID source

DecisionWe looked at a turnkey KYC vendor (Sumsub) and a direct integration with Ghana's National Identification Authority, and chose NIA's remote facial verification.

Trade-offMore authoritative, but far more coordination and paperwork than a plug-in vendor.

Production on AWS, not Vercel

DecisionThe vendor proposed Vercel. I moved production deployment to AWS so environment issues are settled before handoff for testing.

Trade-offMore setup up front for fewer surprises in testing.

A clear line between HYRO and the vendor

DecisionHYRO owns KYC, AML rules, payments, on/off-ramp, settlement, authentication, backend and integrations. Flexx owns the exchange engine, swaps and trading, and parts of the frontend.

Trade-offClear accountability, but more handoffs to manage, so I kept the internal team building rather than waiting on vendor deliverables.

Cost versus scope on integrations

DecisionCompared vendor-proposed providers with in-country alternatives, and swapped in lower-cost options only where they cleared our security and quality bar.

Trade-offMore evaluation time for a lower running cost.

04Execution

The onboarding flow we're building toward

  1. A new user signs up
  2. They take a live selfie
  3. The selfie is checked against national identity records
  4. HYRO receives the result
  5. Verified users continue, and failed checks stop access

What I did

  • Set up MVP scope, roadmap, governance and the 60+ item backlog
  • Ran the NIA process: sent the team to learn the process, prepared the application and organisation profile, chose the service, and put the API into the next sprint
  • Took a stalled Data Protection registration from application to a provisional certificate
  • Wrote the compliance module requirements: KYC tiers, risk assessment, AML monitoring, alerts and audit trails
  • Led vendor reviews and kept responsibilities clear
  • Built an AI workflow that turns meeting notes into Jira tasks, Confluence pages and Slack reminders

What the team did

  • The product manager and developers designed the detailed KYC user flow
  • Developers built the integrations
  • Founders own the vision, fundraising and regulatory relationships
  • Flexx builds the exchange engine and trading

Where it stands: HYRO is pre-launch with a waitlist. The frontend shows the journey with simulated data. Next, the backend verification connects to the frontend once the vendor delivers the UI, and then the product manager and I test the full KYC flow end to end.

06What I learned

  • In regulated fintech, the critical path runs through compliance and third parties, not the codebase.
  • Sequence around dependency readiness, not feature lists. Build what you can while you unblock what you can't.
  • A stalled dependency needs an owner. The Data Protection registration moved once someone took it end to end.
