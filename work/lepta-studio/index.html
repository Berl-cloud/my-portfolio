Turning a feature-rich studio platform into a clear offer for photographers

Lepta Studio gives photographers and studios one place for their portfolio, private client galleries, invoices, payments and an online store. I've been its freelance product manager since July 2026, shaping it into something a busy photographer can pick up quickly.

01The problem

A working photographer runs a small business across too many tools: a website for the portfolio, a separate service for client galleries, payment links, invoices in a spreadsheet, and messages everywhere. Clients get a different experience every time.

Lepta Studio covers all of it, plus stories, a public Explore feed, community challenges and promotions. That breadth was also the problem. The product needed a clear order and a simpler first experience.

Who it's for

  • Solo photographers and filmmakers publishing work and serving clients
  • Studios and agencies with several team members
  • Clients who review, favourite, download and pay
  • Community members entering challenges

02Discovery

The CEO's vision is a calm, branded home for presenting work, serving clients and running the business side of a studio. I tested the product from both sides, as the photographer and as their client, and shared where it asked too much of either.

The biggest insight: there are two users in every job. The photographer wants control and speed. The client wants a private gallery that just works. Both have to feel simple.

03Decisions and trade-offs

Publish and deliver first

DecisionThe MVP is professional publishing and private delivery: studio profile, uploads, public and private albums, sharing, portfolio, clients and invoices. Payments, stories, Explore, challenges and the store follow in Release 1, team features in Release 2, and agency scale in Release 3.

Trade-offCommunity and commerce features wait, so a new photographer can get value on day one.

Creators own their work

DecisionUploaded work is never used to train AI, and album privacy applies everywhere, including thumbnails, search and notifications.

Trade-offSome discovery and AI features are off the table, in exchange for trust.

Lepta never holds client money

DecisionClient payments go through the connected payment provider, which pays the photographer directly.

Trade-offPayment coverage depends on the provider, but Lepta never sits in the money flow.

Price by how a studio grows

DecisionTiers step up by albums, storage and seats, from a free single user to a 20-seat agency.

Trade-offA clear upgrade path, with unlimited plans protected by fair-use limits.

04Execution

  1. Understand

    Clarified the vision and the job each feature does for a photographer or client.

  2. Document

    Wrote the PRD and user stories, with acceptance criteria for every requirement.

  3. Test and simplify

    Tested as both photographer and client, and fed back simpler flows.

  4. Go to market

    Redefined the pricing tiers and wrote the marketing strategy.

What I did

  • Documented the product requirements and user stories that guide testing
  • Set the release plan: MVP, then Releases 1 to 3
  • Test the creator and client journeys end to end
  • Redefined the pricing tiers and wrote the marketing strategy
  • Manage the developer's priorities and delivery

What the team did

  • The CEO sets the vision
  • The developer builds and ships the product

05Outcomes

Success measures I set

  • At least 99% of uploads finish processing
  • At least 99% of payments reconciled to their invoice
  • Zero unauthorised exposures of private work
  • New studios publishing an album or portfolio within 24 hours

Where it stands: Live and in early go-to-market.

06What I learned

  • Every job has two users. The photographer and the client both need the product to feel simple.
  • Privacy is a feature, not a setting. Photographers trust the product with work they don't own alone.
  • Order beats breadth. A clear release plan made a big product easier to explain and to sell.
